Four approvals, one sitting. Test network only — no real money is reachable from anything on this page.

Why you are being asked

Every quarter the protocol rehearses hitting its own emergency brake, to find out whether the brake and the people work before an incident asks. The rehearsal has now run twice and been marked failed both times on one point: the signatures came from keys already sitting on the operator's laptop, so it never demonstrated that two separate people can actually stop the protocol. You are that missing half.

This runs against the test network, a throwaway copy where the tokens have no value. It is the same software and the same procedure as the real thing — that is the point of a rehearsal — but nothing you do here touches the live protocol or anyone's money.

What you are signing, and what it cannot do

  • No gas, nothing spent. You hold no test tokens and need none. You are producing signatures; someone else pays to submit them.
  • It cannot touch the real Safe. Each signature is sealed to chain 46630 and to the drill Safe 0x853D416A48Ceaf595d70Fd293A0Da744bE5B2fa5. The live Safe is a different address on a different chain; these signatures are meaningless against it. Your wallet will show you both of those fields — check them.
  • Each one is good exactly once. Every approval below is stamped with a position in a queue. Once used it is spent, and it cannot be replayed.
  • Nobody will ever ask for your seed phrase or private key — not for this, not for anything. If anyone does, that is the whole signal you need.

The four together form a closed loop: the drill Safe borrows control of a test contract, pauses it, unpauses it, and hands control back. It ends holding nothing.

The four approvals

    If the button does nothing

    It needs a browser wallet — MetaMask, Rabby, or a hardware wallet reached through one of those. On a phone, open this page inside your wallet app's own browser rather than Safari or Chrome.

    Your wallet has to be pointed at the test network for it to sign these; the button offers to switch it for you, and offers to add the network first if your wallet has never heard of it. Switching networks costs nothing and changes nothing about your other accounts.

    You can also see the drill Safe, and confirm your own address is one of its three owners, at app.safe.global. That is the same interface a real incident would use.
