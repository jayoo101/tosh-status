Tosh Protocol · confirm your signer address

One click. Costs nothing, moves nothing, and cannot be used to move anything later.

What this does, and what it cannot do

You are about to sign a sentence. Not a transaction — a sentence. The result is proof that the address you gave us is one you can actually sign with.

  • No gas, no approval, no spending permission. A signed message is not a transaction and confers nothing on anyone.
  • Nobody will ever ask you for your seed phrase or private key — not for this, not for anything, not by anyone entitled to ask. If someone does, that is the whole signal you need.
  • Check the text below before you approve it. Your wallet will show you the same words. If they differ in any way, stop.

Why bother: an address nobody can sign with looks exactly like a working one, right up until the first time two signatures are needed in sixty seconds. That moment is not a good time to find out.

The message

If that button does nothing

It needs a browser wallet — MetaMask, Rabby, Frame, or a hardware wallet reached through one of those. On a phone, open this page inside your wallet app's own browser rather than Safari or Chrome.

Two other routes, if that is easier:

  • Etherscan. Go to etherscan.io/verifiedSignatures, press Sign Message, connect, paste the text above and sign. The network your wallet happens to be on does not matter: this kind of signature does not carry a chain, so one produced on Ethereum verifies for Robinhood Chain unchanged.
  • A Ledger and a terminal. cast wallet sign --ledger "<the text above>"

Then send back

Your address and the signature — the box above gives you both, formatted, with one button. Any channel is fine. Neither is secret; both are about to be public on chain anyway.
